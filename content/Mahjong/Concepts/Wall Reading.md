## Overview
When building a hand, sometimes you are caught having to make a decision between two identical blocks. Guessing what tiles are left in the wall can give you valuable information to make this decision

![[wall_reading_02.png]]

I have two doras here so I want to riichi as fast as possible. Do I go for it even on the one out penchan ![[Man3.svg|20]], or wait for a better wait using the souzu shape? One of the fundamentals of wall reading is looking at the early discards of your opponents. In mahjong discards usually start with lone honors/winds and 1/9 because they have the least possibility of growing into a block. Therefore, you can deduce that number tiles close to early discards are still in the wall. <br>
Going back to the above example, all three opponents are either discarding a ![[Man2.svg|20]] or ![[Man3.svg|20]] on the early turns. They probably didn't have any other low manzu in their hands, so the ![[Man3.svg|20]] might actually be a good wait!

![[wall_reading_02b.png]]
All three of them happened to be left in the wall in this one.

Here's another example where I have a penchan to fill, this time a ![[Pin7.svg|20]]. Here again All three opponents have discarded the higher pinzu so the ![[Pin7.svg|20]] looks like a good tile to wait for.

![[wall_reading_01b.png]]

Kami actually had a ![[Pin5.svg|20]]![[Pin7.svg|20]]![[Pin7.svg|20]]![[Pin9.svg|20]] shape here, so two of the ![[Pin7.svg|20]]s were taken. As you can see, wall reading is not a for sure thing by any means. It's also worth noting that in the vast majority of cases the kanchan is better than the penchan because of it's potential to become a ryanmen. Wall reading can be something handy to know about but just keep in mind that you can never be 100% sure about which tiles are still in the wall and which are not.
