## The Three Conditions
In the most general explanation, calling increases your hand speed and decreases your defense. Three main conditions should be considered when calling a tile. If it satisfies more than two of these, go for it. If if only satisfies one, then it's probably not a good decision. Anything in between is hard.

> 1. The hand has speed
> 2. The hand has value
> 3. The hand retains defense

Think of having four blocks and a head as a baseline for hands with speed. In the above example I have 5 distinct blocks, two of which are ryanmen, and a pair of ![[Man4.svg|20]] as my head. I also have a ![[Chun.svg|20]]as a defensive tile that I can keep in my hand so this satisfies conditions 1 and 3. Calling the ![[Pin2.svg|20]] before the ![[Hatsu.svg|20]] does two things: it gives me two honors to use for defense against an early opponent riichi, but leaves the hand yaku nashi. If I complete both ryanmens before calling ![[Hatsu.svg|20]], my hand collapses into  a ![[Man4.svg|20]]![[Hatsu.svg|20]] shanpon, effectively cutting my wait in half. I decided to take this risk because I have a lot of pairs and calling pon on them can really speed up my hand.

## Calling into Tenpai
The priority in mahjong is to go for the riichi, but there are times where calling is the right decision if it puts you in tenpai.

![[call_decision_03.png]]

By calling this ![[Man7.svg|20]] I can secure tenpai waiting on ![[Sou8.svg|20]]. Since junchan is a restrictive yaku, I cannot afford to be picky about how I enter tenpai.
