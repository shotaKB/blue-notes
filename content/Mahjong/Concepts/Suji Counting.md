## Overview
You want to be decisive when it comes to pushing or folding. Most of the time it is better to choose one and then commit to it until the end. However mahjong is not always that simple, and you will encounter borderline situations from time to time. One technique that can help you navigate those is suji counting.

Because of the furiten rule, if a player discards a tile, they cannot win on that same tile using a two-sided wait (ryanmen). For example if ![[Sou4.svg|20]] is discarded, that player cannot win on ![[Sou1.svg|20]]-![[Sou4.svg|20]] or ![[Sou4.svg|20]]-![[Sou7.svg|20]] waits. Those are sujis. There are six sujis per suit (![[Sou1.svg|20]]-![[Sou4.svg|20]], ![[Sou4.svg|20]]-![[Sou7.svg|20]], ![[Sou2.svg|20]]-![[Sou5.svg|20]], ![[Sou5.svg|20]]-![[Sou8.svg|20]], ![[Sou3.svg|20]]-![[Sou6.svg|20]], and ![[Sou6.svg|20]]-![[Sou9.svg|20]]) and 3 suits, totaling 18 sujis. If you look at an opponent riichi and count 15 sujis left, then any random discard you make will have a small percentage of dealing in. However if there are only 5 sujis left, then that same discard becomes much more dangerous.

## Example

![[suji_counting_001.png]]

I definitely don't want to deal in to this oya riichi but I am tenpai myself. The hand value is only [[Simple Scoring Chart|2000]] but I have a good wait. Let's try to push this as far as we can. Looking at the opponent's discards, I see four number tiles which eliminates four sujis: ![[Man1.svg|20]]-![[Man4.svg|20]], ![[Sou1.svg|20]]-![[Sou4.svg|20]], ![[Man5.svg|20]]-![[Man8.svg|20]], and ![[Pin5.svg|20]]-![[Pin8.svg|20]]. That's still 14 sujis left, so there is a good chance this ![[Pin2.svg|20]] passes.

![[suji_counting_002.png]]

I pulled another musuji, so let's pause. In addition to the four aforementioned sujis, I see three more new ones: ![[Sou2.svg|20]]-![[Sou5.svg|20]], ![[Pin1.svg|20]]-![[Pin4.svg|20]], and ![[Man2.svg|20]]-![[Man5.svg|20]]. This ![[Pin4.svg|20]] will be the eighth, getting less safe but not enough to give up on my own ryanmen wait.

![[suji_counting_003.png]]

Now the alarm bells are ringing with this pull. Not only are there nine sujis left, this tile actually accounts for two of them because neither ![[Sou3.svg|20]]-![[Sou6.svg|20]] nor ![[Sou6.svg|20]]-![[Sou9.svg|20]] has been discarded. It's twice as dangerous as the other discards and I've decided that this is where I [[Folding|fold]].

## Notes
Keep in mind that suji counting only accounts for ryanmen waits because those are the most common in mahjong. Both the ![[Pin2.svg|20]] and the ![[Pin4.svg|20]] I discarded could have been hit on kanchan or other waits. If your hand is terrible, then the wise thing to do is to look for genbutsu and hard fold.
