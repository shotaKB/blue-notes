*The Geography of Thought* <br>
Richard E. Nisbett

I had a long discussion with a coworker about kindness. I always try to be helpful to everyone I come across because that has always been the right thing to do for me and I feel like the society expects me to do so. My coworker, on the other hand, could not understand why I would treat some random stranger and someone close to me, like a mother, the same way. It was a deeply thought provoking experience because I've never had my idea of kindness get challenged like that. It ended up getting me in a deep dive of Western vs Eastern thought, what ideas each society is based on, and how that affects our actions.

The book focuses mainly on how logic is formed in Western (primarily the U.S. and Europe) and Asian countries. It really doesn't get into the emotional parts like kindness. But as an Asian raised in the U.S., the studies in this book was still very interesting. There were parts of it I could understand both points of views, such as associating a cow with a chicken or with grass. Other parts were completely eye opening, like how people write essays in a completely different style in Asian countries. The only way I know is the "standard" writing style: thesis -> provide evidence -> explore counter arguments -> restate thesis. I just took for granted that this is how everybody writes academic papers, although it's worth noting that this book is nearly 20 years old and things could have changed within that timeframe. Overall this book got me to organize some details that were confusing me while living in Japan and gave me a better idea of what the people I interact with every day base their actions on.
