*やがて魔女の森になる* [Slowly Becoming the Witch's Forest] <br>
Kawaguchi Harumi

I was waiting for a friend in Shimokitazawa one afternoon. She was running late so I randomly started walking around until I found a tiny gallery. Well there was nothing in the gallery, but upstairs was a complete house of books from ceiling to floor. I figured this was a small private business because the selection of books was heavily skewed towards art, music, poetry, and other miraculous topics that will hardly ever crack the popular books top 50. I saw the entire god damn back shelf was poetry publications, and I knew I found Arcadia.

I'm just starting to get back into reading more poetry. I didn't know the author, nor the integrity of the award this book won. It starts off stranded in a ship with scared people. The setting felt very much like a fantasy world, then quickly dove into cityscapes, a bed on the streets, and schoolyards. I was honestly caught off guard by how modern they were. The author didn't coat the hardships in a mystical setting, but rather laid it naked and bare. If I want to internalize pain, this is where to come back to.
