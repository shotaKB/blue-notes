*Galápagos* <br>
Kurt Vonnegut

One of those postmodern, "what the fuck is even going on" kind of books. It was much more comprehensible than *Gravity's Rainbow* because here we actually have a "ghost" narrator instead of a free for all. The time line is also linear, from the global crisis to the end of humanity, unlike in *Catch-22* where anyone mentioning "loyalty" can cause an erratic jump cut. This book has it's own confusions, of course. Furries, the constant Mandarax quotations, the narrators past...

People having "big brains" sounded like a sci-fi setting in the beginning, something that might happen in the near future. But then here we are trying to create artificial intelligence chat bots and trying to extract emotions (something that hits the heart directly) to turn into numbers, so maybe we really do have too big of a brain.
